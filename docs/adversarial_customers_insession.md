# Adversarial review — Customer & unit-economics POV (in-session pass)

Two characters play hostile. The strongest objections, then which are fatal vs design-solvable.

## Character 1a — SF café/bar owner (Mission), pitched weekly

- **"Why would I want 30 strangers on a Tuesday for $0 from you?"** Real math: a 30-person meetup that buys 1.5 drinks avg at $8–12 = $360–540 revenue, ~70% gross margin = $250–380 gross profit. A normal dead Tuesday evening might do $150–250 revenue. So the honest pitch is "roughly double a dead night", not "fill your venue" — overselling this is the #1 way to lose venues forever.
- **Real costs I carry:** one extra staff member ($20–25/h × 4h = $80–100) if the group exceeds my Tuesday staffing; noise complaints from neighbors/regulars; wear. If the event clears <$300 F&B I LOSE money staffing it. → Forces a min-group-size and a spend expectation into the schema.
- **"Dead hours" aren't always inventory.** Tuesday 19–22h I might be prepping, cleaning, hosting staff training, or just *want the room empty*. The schema must capture "actually bookable" slots, not "we're technically open".
- **Disintermediation:** after event #1 I have the organiser's number. Why pay you again? → Only sustainable answer: you bring me *new different* organisers each month (I can't source 6 different book clubs myself) + attribution data + priority placement. If we're a one-time introducer, we're dead.
- **Permits/licensing:** ticketed paid-entry events in a café can trigger entertainment/assembly permits; alcohol license tied to my license type. A private RSVP'd group ordering at the bar = fine. A "ticketed supper club" advertised publicly = grey zone. → v1: events stay "private group bookings", no door tickets sold on premise.

## Character 1b — Málaga centro bar/café owner

- Answers WhatsApp when she feels like it; the channel IS the product. If ops kit isn't WhatsApp-first in ES, conversion halves.
- Economics flip: smaller group spends (coffee+tapas €8–15/head → 25 people ≈ €200–375) but her dead night is ~€80–150, so the same event is 2–3× a dead night — *better* relative pitch than SF.
- Trust > data: neighborhood owner in centro says yes because "el chico que vino" not because of a dashboard. POC should lead with a named event and a date, not a platform.
- Seasonality: Oct–Dec is fine; Aug is a dead month for nomad/extra events (everyone's at the beach or gone). Don't extrapolate Aug data.

## Character 2 — the recurring organiser

- **"Why pay when I can DM cafés free?"** Honest answer: time. A recurring organiser spends 1–3 hrs/event venue-hunting (DMs, dead replies, min-spend negotiation, day-of logistics). If we compress that to 1 message, $25–50/event is cheap — BUT only if our slots are *real and reliable*. A booking that falls through is -$50 + reputation damage with their attendees.
- **Will a café room work for me?** Depends on format: language exchange/board games/casual meetup = yes. Tech talk with projector = no (needs AV + quiet). Yoga/workshop = no (needs floor/quiet). → Format filter matters: ~the POC's eligible formats are social formats, not content formats. The data pack must count them separately.
- **Luma already does my RSVP for free; your fee stacks.** Framing that works: "we're not ticketing, we're the room" — fee is for the venue transaction, comparable to what they'd lose in failed-venue hours, not to Luma's 5%.
- **Disintermediation (the killer):** a *recurring* organiser locks a venue for months. One booking fee then I'm gone for a year. → This is the fatal structural objection to per-booking pricing: revenue ≠ f(events) for recurring organisers, it's f(first-matches). Fixes: (a) charge per-event always (fee every booking, not first) — they accept it only if we keep solving *something* each month: flexible slots, backup venue when theirs is booked, multi-venue rotation their attendees enjoy; (b) min-spend deal negotiated through us makes *us* the reason the deal exists — direct renegotiation loses it; (c) recurring-organiser subscription (the €29–59/mo from pivot A) only works if the service is continuous, not matchmaking.

## Fatal vs solvable — verdict

| Objection | Fatal? | Design response |
|---|---|---|
| Organiser disintermediation after booking #1 | **Near-fatal for per-first-match pricing** | Charge per event, always; make min-spend deals + multi-venue rotation + guaranteed backup slot the ongoing value. If organisers only ever need us once, this is a lead-gen business, not a platform — and pricing must become per-event-fee-heavy fast. |
| Venue disintermediation | Solvable | Venue's ongoing need is *new demand supply* — they can't source organisers; we stay in by controlling demand pipeline. |
| Small-ticket economics (Malaga €200 events) | Serious | Fees must scale: €15–30 flat in Malaga vs $40–75 SF. Take-rate model (%) handles both automatically; flat-fee model doesn't. |
| "Dead hours" not actually bookable | Serious | Schema captures stated-available slots only; verify on walk-in. |
| Ticketed-event licensing | Manageable | v1 = private RSVP'd groups; no on-premise ticket sales. Flag for legal check before any public-ticketing format. |
| Staffing cost kills small events | Solvable | min_group ≥ 20–25 or spend floor in schema. |

## Top-3 forced design changes

1. **Fee model: per-event always (not first-match), % or flat-with-floor** — the business only works if every event pays; the recurring-organiser subscription must be earned by continuous service, not assumed.
2. **Format filter in the product**: eligible formats = social (language, book club, supper club, games, run-club social, networking). Content formats needing AV/quiet are a different supply type (coworking/event space) — don't mix them in the POC metrics.
3. **"Actually bookable" is the only inventory that counts**: slots in the schema must be owner-confirmed bookable windows, not opening hours. First-walk-in script must extract real slots, not "we're open till 11".
